What stays fixed, what people can change, and what Free sees

The rules that keep every Studio page trustworthy and, in the owner’s words of 11 Nov, make sure people “should not be able to make bad designs”: bad designs are impossible by construction, not warned about. And a supporter should recognise TipMe’s tip block, its fees and its report link on any page, however it’s styled. The upload rules below are read from origin/dev today (apps/api/src/profiles/image-upload.ts, cover.controller.ts, avatar.controller.ts; packages/contracts/src/social-links.ts, covers.ts). Proposed

Always TipMe (fixed)

  • The tip block: TipMe’s own colours, fonts and shape, in the place the layout gives it (right column on desktop, straight after the name on a phone). Only its amounts change, through today’s Pro tip amounts.
  • How fees are shown: the same line and the same full total before paying, on every page and theme.
  • The Pro badge: after the handle, as on the ProBadge board. It can’t be moved, recoloured or hidden by a theme.
  • “Report this page” and the footer’s Privacy and Terms links (and “Learn about this preview” when it shows).
  • Name, photo and handle stay at the top, so a page can’t pretend to be someone else’s.
  • Closed tipping, “not taking tips yet” and the pilot notice show exactly as today.

Theirs to change

  • Theme: 8 curated palettes (today’s cover colours plus Night), each with a light and dark version and a small set of tested accents; 3 font pairs (heading and body together) from the two brand fonts; a background from today’s covers, their photo, or an animated cover.
  • Nothing unreadable can be chosen. Every text and background pair in every palette is checked before it ships (4.5 to 1 for text, 3 to 1 for large headings, light and dark). No colour picker, so there is no warning state.
  • Sections, up to 8, each kind once: About, Gallery, Videos, Services and prices, Events, Featured links, Supporter wall, Pinned note. Add, remove, reorder; they snap into the layout’s grid. Each has text limits, and an empty section hides on the public page.
  • Layout: Classic, Portfolio first or Minimal. Sizes, spacing and line lengths come from the layout, never the person.

Never

  • No custom HTML, CSS or JavaScript, and no tracking pixels or third-party scripts.
  • No free colour, font or size picks; no free positioning or resizing; nothing over, inside or in front of the tip block.
  • No second payment button, no “pay for this service”, no goal or target: TipMe takes tips, not orders (as ProAdditions §6, and tip goals stay deferred).

Photos and videos

Exists Photos go through today’s upload, the one page photos and covers use:

  • JPEG, PNG or WebP only, up to 5 MB; SVG never.
  • Re-encoded to a WebP of a fixed shape: square 512 by 512 (as the page photo) or strip 2240 by 600 (as the cover). Gallery tiles use one of the two.
  • All metadata, such as EXIF location, is dropped in the re-encode.
  • Proposed Cropped and fitted to each section’s shape automatically; the person only picks a focal point. Photos below a minimum size are refused with a plain message. Text never sits on a photo: backgrounds behind text are presets with a built-in scrim.

Proposed Videos are embeds from an allow-list only:

  • A pasted link is checked against listed sites, like social links today (sites to be decided: question 2). Anything else is refused: “We can’t show videos from that site yet.”
  • Shown as a still with a play button; the other site loads only after a tap, so visiting a page sends nothing to it.
  • No video uploads to TipMe.

Moderation: “Report this page” and its reasons already exist (PROFILE_REPORT_REASONS in packages/contracts/src/profiles.ts). Whether staff also see new gallery images is question 5.

The promise the build must test

Every combination of palette, font pair and layout renders well from 320 to 1440 pixels wide, with the longest allowed text in every section, empty sections, the largest and smallest photo counts, and 200% zoom. 8 palettes × 2 versions × 3 font pairs × 3 layouts = 144 combinations, each checked automatically for contrast, overflow and overlap before a palette, font pair or layout ships. A combination that fails is fixed or removed, never left for a person to find.
