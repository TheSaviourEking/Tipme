TipMe for businesses: a first sketch to react to

Drawn from your words on 5–6 November 2026: a business has a code for each location; scanning it shows the staff working there; the supporter picks the person and tips them through the usual TipMe flow; a review is optional; the business can propose a share of staff tips, which the staff member has to accept; and the business sees who is tipped most and what customers say. Everything here is Proposed; nothing is built. Items marked Owner to decide need your answer. Nothing here is legal advice or implies a regulatory approval.

1. Codes: one per location, staff codes optional

One code per location keeps printing simple: a table tent, a counter card or a sign at the till. Staff can still have their own card when the business wants it, for example a waiter’s badge.

A badge or card for one person: it opens straight onto their tip page at that business, skipping the list.

Uses the staff member’s existing TipMe page and QR. When they leave, the business removes them and the badge stops showing the business.

Owner to decide

Who can a code show?

  • Proposed: staff “on today” at that location, set by the manager or by the staff member checking in.
  • Simpler first version: everyone at that location, always.

5. How it fits TipMe today

Proposed

Staff are ordinary TipMe users

  • Each staff member keeps their own TipMe account, balance and withdrawals. Joining a business adds them to its location pages; leaving removes them.
  • The business is a new kind of account with managers, locations and staff invitations (the product document’s Phase 2, FR-08).
  • The business’s share, if any, goes to the business’s own balance.
Before building

What to settle first

  • Our payment partner’s view on paying a business a share of staff tips, and on business accounts.
  • Whether the business pays for TipMe (a plan), takes no share, or both.
  • The faster tipping flow you asked about applies here too: it gets its own board next.
