When something goes wrong, or right, it should still feel like TipMe.

Today every message is the same tinted box with a “!” or “✓” in front. That covers 105 messages: 71 errors, 15 neutral notes, 8 successes, and 11 that take the default tone or get theirs as the page runs. Below are two ways to give them TipMe’s own materials, applied to the same six real messages. The words are today’s text unless a purple Proposed tag says otherwise.

Concept A

The slip

A message is a paper slip that comes out from under the thing it’s about, like the receipt from a till. A small ink stamp says what happened, and the way out is a real key on the slip.

Concept B

The key tells you

The state lives in the control itself. The key you pressed shows the result on its own face, with a small lamp (red, amber or purple) and the way out built into it. The explanation is one plain line, with no box.

My recommendation: both, each where it fits

  • The key tells you for quick results right at a button: saves, copy link, “Saved.”, Try again on a dropped connection. Nothing to read, the key you pressed answers.
  • The slip when there’s something to explain or check: money (withdrawals, tips not sent), account safety, and staff actions. The stamp is readable at a glance, and the slip has room for what’s safe and what to do.
  • Field errors use B’s red edge inside the well, with the reason beside the number.
  • Empty states use A’s blank slip on public and recipient pages, and B’s unlit well in the admin area.

Rules for both

  • No tinted alert boxes, no “!” or “✓” in front of words.
  • Colour is never the only signal: every state has words.
  • A reassuring line (“You haven’t paid anything”, “the money is back in your balance”) appears only where the code proves it.
  • Errors are still announced (role alert) and successes politely (role status), as today.
